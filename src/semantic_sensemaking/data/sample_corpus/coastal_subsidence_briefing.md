# Coastal subsidence briefing (synthetic example)

This briefing is synthetic text written for the semantic-sensemaking examples.

Along the coast, land subsidence and sea-level rise act together. Where the ground
settles by a few millimetres each year, relative sea level rises faster than the
global average, and tidal flooding reaches streets that stayed dry a decade ago.
Much of the settlement traces back to groundwater pumping: as aquifers are drawn
down, clay layers lose water and compaction becomes permanent.

City engineers now combine InSAR time series from Sentinel-1 with continuous GPS
stations to map where the shoreline infrastructure is sinking fastest. The
interferogram stack shows a subsidence bowl beneath the port and the older
metropolitan districts, where storm surge and inundation risk are highest.

The planning office wants a decision support tool that links these observations
to flood maps, so stakeholders can compare pumping limits, levee upgrades, and
managed retreat for the salt marsh along the estuary.
